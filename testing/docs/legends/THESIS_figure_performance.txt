Figure: genome-wide CPA identification, every model against the baseline
Methods, numbers, results, and the figure caption
========================================================================

The figure places the sequence CNN and the splicing-feature models on one axis against a defined
baseline, on a single held-out split, with one metric declared primary.


PART I   METHODS
================

I.1  TASK AND WHY THE PRIMARY METRIC IS WITHIN-GENE
----------------------------------------------------
The task is to identify the real 3' end among the candidate peaks of a gene. Each gene contributes
one real 3' end and its own competitors, which are filtered baseline-model false positives.

The primary metric is the WITHIN-GENE WIN RATE: for each gene, the fraction of its competitors that
the model ranks below the real 3' end, ties counted as 0.5, then averaged over genes. It answers the
question the task actually asks and it is invariant to any per-gene shift in score.

Pooled AUROC and AUPRC treat all 14,198 peaks as one list. They therefore also reward a model for
separating gene A's peaks from gene B's, which the task never asks about. They are reported for
comparability with other work and are marked "reference" on the figure.

The disagreement between the two is not a technicality and is why both are drawn. See II.3.

I.2  DATA
---------
All models are scored on the same held-out split: 1,623 genes, 14,198 peaks, of which 1,623 are real
3' ends and 12,575 are competitors, a base rate of 0.1143. The split is by gene, so no gene
contributes to both training and test.

I.3  THE BASELINE
-----------------
Chance, computed analytically rather than simulated, and drawn both as a row and as the dashed
reference line in every panel:

  win rate   0.5000    by definition
  P@1        0.2923    the mean over test genes of 1 / (1 + number of competitors)
  AUROC      0.5000    by definition
  AUPRC      0.1143    the base rate

P@1's chance value is not 0 and not 0.5; it depends on how many competitors each gene has, which is
why it is computed from the test genes rather than assumed.

I.4  THE MODELS
---------------
Every row is scored on the split above. The file records for each row whether it was refitted,
rescored from locked coefficients, or read from saved per-peak scores.

  acceptor 3'ss          1 feature.   Maximum MaxEntScan score3 upstream of the peak.
  window GC              1 feature.   GC fraction in [-500, 0).
  GC dip depth           1 feature.   Depth of the GC dip in each site's own frame.
  splice, scalars        3 features.  Scalar splice summaries.
  splice, positional    51 features.  Binned U1 and acceptor features, scored from locked
                                      coefficients rather than refitted.
  splice, binned       126 features.  The full binned splice set.
  splice + dip depth    52 features.  Positional splice plus the GC dip scalar.
  splice + GC per bin   76 features.  Positional splice plus positional composition. A different
                                      axis from the rows above: splice biology plus composition.
  boosted tree          51 features.  Gradient-boosted trees on the positional splice features.
  sequence CNN                        Per-peak scores from cnn_model_scores.npz, not refitted.

Two rows are marked as illustrations rather than ladder steps, drawn with open marks: window GC,
which is a pooled-versus-within-gene illustration, and the boosted tree, for the reason in II.3.

I.5  INTERVALS
--------------
Within-gene win rate carries 95% intervals bootstrapped over genes. P@1 and the pooled metrics are
drawn as points; their intervals are in cnn_metrics.tsv for the CNN rows and are omitted here to
keep four panels legible.

II.2  WHAT THE LADDER SHOWS
---------------------------
The acceptor alone is worth +0.120 over chance, and it is the single strongest one-feature splice
signal in the set. Three splice scalars reach 0.666 and the positional treatment 0.696, so most of
the splice signal is in WHERE the sites are rather than how strong they are: going from 3 scalars to
51 positional features buys +0.030, while the acceptor's own strength buys +0.120 from nothing.

Adding positional composition to positional splice gives the best engineered model, 0.740. That row
is a different axis from the ones below it and is labelled as such.

The sequence CNN reaches 0.8211 [0.8062, 0.8364], which is +0.081 over the best engineered model and
+0.321 over chance. Its interval does not overlap that of any other row. Read as precision at 1, it
puts the real 3' end at the top of its gene in 64.8% of genes against 51.8% for the best engineered
model and 29.2% for chance.

So roughly 75% of the total gain over chance is reached by hand-built splice and composition
features, and the CNN adds the remaining quarter from sequence alone.

II.3  WHY BOTH METRIC FAMILIES ARE DRAWN
-----------------------------------------
The boosted tree is the case that justifies the whole convention. It reaches a pooled AUROC of 0.858
and a pooled AUPRC of 0.508, both far above every other row including the CNN's 0.818 and 0.449. On
the primary metric it sits at 0.683, BELOW the ridge on the same 51 features at 0.696.

The tree is reconstructing gene-level context, which separates genes from one another and inflates
any pooled metric, while gene-centring removes exactly that. A reader shown only panels C and D
would conclude the boosted tree is the best model in the figure. It is not the best model for this
task.

The same row is also the only one sensitive to input storage precision: 0.6834 on float64 inputs
against 0.6797 if the same channels are read back from their float32 cache, where the ridge gives
0.6957 either way.

II.4  WHAT THIS FIGURE DOES NOT SHOW
-------------------------------------
It compares models at ranking candidates WITHIN a gene, on peaks that a baseline model already
proposed. It is not a measure of de novo genome-wide discovery, since the candidate set is given.
The competitors are that baseline model's false positives, so the task is deliberately the hard
residual it leaves behind rather than the easy separation it already achieves.
