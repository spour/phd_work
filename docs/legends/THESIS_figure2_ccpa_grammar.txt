--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CAPTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Figure X. Competitor CPA (cCPA) sites carry the same cleavage-and-polyadenylation
grammar as canonical 3' ends. All panels describe the 43,547 analysis-set cCPA
sites (intragenic positions that every model scored at or above their gene's own
annotated 3' end) and the 6,583 canonical sites, genome-wide, from cross-fitted
model predictions. (A) Inter-model peak convergence: the spread (maximum minus
minimum) of the random-forest, logistic-regression and CNN peak centres for each
cCPA site (n = 43,547). The three independently trained models place the site
within a few nucleotides of each other (median 5 nt; 80.4% within 50 nt, 18.5%
beyond 60 nt), so a cCPA call is a property of the sequence rather than of one
architecture. (B) Within-gene rank of the canonical site among its own gene's cCPA
sites under APARENT2, ties counted as half, binned by the number of cCPA sites in
the gene (n genes above each point; 4,332 genes; error bars are 95% CIs, dashed
line is no preference). A gene with n cCPA sites can only produce ranks on a
lattice of n + 1 values, so a raw histogram spikes at 0.5 and 1.0 for arithmetic
reasons; binning by contest size shows the statistic sits near 0.5 at every size
(overall 0.477, 95% CI 0.465-0.489). (C) Positional density of PAS hexamers per
peak (AATAAA plus the 12 commonest single-substitution variants) in a +/-100 nt
strand-oriented window, for real sequence (solid) and a dinucleotide-preserving
shuffle null (dashed, 20 Altschul-Erikson shuffles per peak); 9-nt moving average.
Both classes show the same sharp PAS peak at -23 nt, ~8-fold
above the composition-matched null, so the signal is a positioned hexamer and not
dinucleotide-composition noise (PAS present in the -50..0 zone: 96.1% of canonical
and 98.8% of cCPA peaks, versus 37.9% and 35.3% under shuffle). (D) Pooled
APARENT2 ROC for discriminating canonical from cCPA sites and (E) the matching
precision-recall curve, over the 5,898 canonical and 38,186 cCPA peaks with an
APARENT2 score. APARENT2 does not separate the classes (AUROC 0.478, 95% CI
0.470-0.485, chance 0.500; AUPRC 0.134, 95% CI 0.129-0.138, chance 0.134). E is the
informative panel: canonical sites are 13.4% of the set, so the no-skill AUPRC is
0.134, not 0.5. (F) Positional density per peak of four CPA motif classes, on
independent y scales: PAS hexamers, CFIm/UGUA (TGTA), U-rich (T) and GU-rich
(GT/TG), same window and smoothing as C. Canonical and cCPA profiles are
superimposable in shape and position, including the upstream PAS peak and the
downstream U-rich/GU-rich element; the only visible class difference is a modestly
lower upstream UGUA density at cCPA sites (mean count 0.68 versus 0.84 per peak).
Blue, canonical 3' ends; orange, cCPA sites throughout.

Interpretive caveat to keep with the figure: every panel is descriptive sequence
composition, and the sites were selected because sequence models scored them at or
above the gene's own canonical site. Sequence competence here is a property of the
construction of the set, not independent evidence that these sites are used.

SHORT VARIANT

Figure X. cCPA sites are sequence-competent. Blue, canonical 3' ends (n = 6,583);
orange, analysis-set cCPA sites (n = 43,547); genome-wide, cross-fitted
predictions. (A) Spread of the random-forest, logistic-regression and CNN peak
centres per cCPA site: median 5 nt, 80.4% within 50 nt. (B) APARENT2 within-gene
rank of the canonical site among its own gene's cCPA sites, binned by contest size
(4,332 genes, 95% CIs, dashed line = no preference); overall 0.477 [0.465, 0.489].
(C) PAS hexamer density per peak, +/-100 nt, real (solid) versus a
dinucleotide-preserving shuffle null (dashed, 20 shuffles per peak), 9-nt moving
average: both classes show the same upstream peak far above the null. (D, E)
Pooled APARENT2 ROC and precision-recall over the 5,898 canonical and 38,186 cCPA
peaks with a score: AUROC 0.478 [0.470, 0.485] and AUPRC 0.134 [0.129, 0.138]
against a 0.134 baseline. (F) Positional density of PAS, UGUA, U-rich and GU-rich
motifs on independent y scales; the two classes are superimposable. The sites were
selected by sequence models, so this competence is a property of the set's
construction, not evidence of use.

Upstream inputs
  shortlist_crossfit_windows.parquet  peaks + rf_center / lr_center / cnn_center
  shortlist_cpa.parquet               50,130 rows = 43,547 cCPA + 6,583 canonical,
                                      motif counts, dinuc-null counts, aparent2_prob
  cpa_peaks_final.parquet             in_analysis_set filter (--peaks)
  ../train/data/hg38.fa               sequence for the +/-100 nt windows

Parameters
  window +/-100 nt strand-oriented; density smoothed with a 9-point moving average in R
  null: 20 dinucleotide-preserving (Altschul-Erikson 1985) shuffles per peak, seed 0
  PAS = AATAAA + ATTAAA TATAAA AGTAAA AAGAAA AATATA AATACA CATAAA GATAAA AATGAA
        TTTAAA ACTAAA AATAGA   (Beaudoing 2000)
  UGUA = TGTA; U-rich = T positions; GU-rich = GT/TG dinucleotides
  scalar summaries: PAS zone = -50..0, DSE = 0..+50

Rebuild
  python3 scripts/figure2_stats.py --shortlist shortlist_crossfit_windows.parquet \
      --cpa shortlist_cpa.parquet --peaks cpa_peaks_final.parquet \
      --cpa_bg_auroc 0.991 --out .
  Rscript scripts/make_figure2.R .

Two asymmetries to be aware of before quoting n values
  1. The cCPA class is the 43,547 analysis-set competitors, but the canonical class
     is all 6,583 genes, not the 4,847 that survive the funnel in
     figure_analysis_set_funnel. Panels C and F therefore average over 50,130 peaks.
  2. Panels D and E drop peaks with no APARENT2 score (38,186 of 43,547 cCPA and
     5,898 of 6,583 canonical), so their n is smaller than every other panel's.
