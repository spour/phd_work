CAPTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Figure X. Published 3' end datasets agree only modestly with each other and with
model calls, once gene coverage is held fixed. Five sets of 3' end positions are
compared: the canonical 3' end of each gene, the internal competitors (peaks the
random forest ranks above the canonical site of the same gene), the 3'-seq
intronic polyadenylation atlas (Singh 2018 merged with Lee 2018), sites of
telescripting loss under U1 AMO treatment (Oh 2017), and PCF11-dependent premature
CPA sites. All panels are restricted to the 334 protein-coding genes in which all
five sets place at least one site (6,814 sites: 356 canonical, 4,027 internal
competitors, 573 3'-seq atlas, 552 PCF11-dependent, 1,306 U1 AMO). The restriction
is the point of the figure: low overlap between two datasets otherwise admits two
explanations, genuine disagreement about where 3' ends lie and simply having
surveyed different genes, and the unrestricted comparison cannot separate them.
(A) Empirical cumulative distribution of the distance from each site of the source
set (one facet per source, header giving its site count) to the nearest site of
each target set in the same gene; colour is the target set, the dashed line marks
the 50 nt matching tolerance used throughout, and the x axis is logarithmic.
Matching is within gene rather than within chromosome, since two sites in different
genes are not evidence that two datasets found the same 3' end. (B) Pairwise
containment at 50 nt: C(A into B), the fraction of the row set's sites lying within
50 nt of any site of the column set. The measure is asymmetric, because the sets
differ in size by an order of magnitude, and is read across a row; the diagonal is
undefined. Holding the gene scope fixed raises containment between the external
sets from 0.004-0.094 to 0.161-0.237, so most of the apparent disagreement between
these published datasets was differing gene coverage rather than differing calls.
Agreement is nonetheless modest in absolute terms: even for the best-agreeing pair,
roughly three quarters of one set's sites have no counterpart within 50 nt in the other
(3'-seq atlas into competitors 0.237, PCF11-dependent into 3'-seq atlas 0.225,
3'-seq atlas into PCF11-dependent 0.215). (C) Percentage of each set's sites
carrying AATAAA or ATTAAA within the 60 nt upstream, read on the annotated strand
(red), against the same sites translated 5 kb along the chromosome (grey), a
positional control that preserves local isochore and repeat context while
destroying the relationship to the 3' end; the printed fold change is observed over
displaced. Every set but one is strongly PAS-enriched (internal competitors 95.5%,
11.3-fold; canonical 86.0%, 12.8-fold; 3'-seq atlas 80.1%, 9.0-fold;
PCF11-dependent 66.3%, 8.3-fold), whereas the U1 AMO set is not (13.9%, 1.5-fold).
(D) Distribution of baseline model scores at each set's sites, taken as the maximum
over every model window whose 500 nt span covers the site, so a site scores low only
if all covering windows do. Left, calibrated random-forest probability from the
per-nucleotide genome scan; right, CNN percentile of the out-of-fold logit against
all 70,723 scored windows. Violins show the full distribution, boxes the
interquartile range and median, and the printed number is the median. Neither model
saw the three external datasets in training, and both rank U1 AMO lowest of them
(forest 3'-seq atlas 0.84 > PCF11-dependent 0.81 > U1 AMO 0.71; CNN
PCF11-dependent 0.75 > 3'-seq atlas 0.64 > U1 AMO 0.63). Logistic regression is
absent: its scores survive without coordinates, so it cannot be placed on a genomic
site without retraining.

Read panel D's competitor row with care. Competitors were defined as peaks the
forest ranks above the canonical site of the same gene, so that row is circular for
both models. The selection is a within-gene statement and panel D is a marginal one
pooled over genes, and the two need not agree: within gene on this scope the best
competitor still outscores the canonical site in 71.9% of genes under the forest,
while the CNN reverses that to 44.6% (57.7% canonical-first over all 6,583 genes),
which is what the CNN was trained to do. The three external sets are the honest
test.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SHORT VARIANT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Figure X. Concordance of published 3' end datasets with each other and with model
calls. Five site sets (canonical 3' ends, internal competitors, 3'-seq IpA atlas,
PCF11-dependent CPA, U1 AMO), restricted to the 334 protein-coding genes in which
all five place a site (6,814 sites; counts in the panel A facet headers). (A) ECDF
of the distance from each source-set site to the nearest site of each target set in
the same gene; dashed line, the 50 nt tolerance. (B) Containment at 50 nt: the
fraction of the row set within 50 nt of the column set, asymmetric, read across a
row. Fixing the gene scope lifts external-set containment from 0.004-0.094 to
0.161-0.237, so most apparent disagreement was gene coverage; agreement is still
modest: even the best pair matches only about a quarter of one set. (C) Sites carrying
AATAAA or ATTAAA within 60 nt upstream (red) against the same sites displaced 5 kb
(grey), with fold change. All sets are PAS-enriched 8.3- to 12.8-fold except U1
AMO (13.9%, 1.5-fold). (D) Per-site maximum random-forest probability and CNN
percentile; medians printed. Both models rank U1 AMO lowest of the three external
sets. The competitor row is circular for both models, since competitors were
selected by the forest's within-gene ranking.

PROVENANCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Plotting        make_figure_overlap_shared.R   -> figure_overlap_shared.pdf/.png
                (reads only TSVs; computes nothing but the ECDFs)
Panels A-C      overlap_shared_recompute.py    -> shared_distances.tsv,
                                                  shared_containment.tsv,
                                                  shared_hexamer.tsv
Panel D         overlap_scores_sites.tsv (CNN percentile, per site, in_shared flag)
                overlap_rf_dense.tsv     (rf_dense_max from the genome scan)
Gene scope      overlap_shared_genes.tsv (334 genes)
Sequence        /scratch/spour98/jobs/hg38.fa (+ .fai) for the hexamer test
Companion text  figure_overlap_shared_notes.txt  (full methods, results, limits)

Parameters
  TOL   = 50 nt matching tolerance (panel A dashed line, panel B threshold)
  UP    = 60 nt upstream window for AATAAA / ATTAAA
  SHIFT = 5,000 nt positional control displacement
  gene assignment by interval containment against GENCODE v43 protein-coding genes

Why these panels were recomputed rather than filtered
  hiconf_out/ holds the unrestricted versions (distances.tsv, jaccard_matrix.tsv,
  hexamer.tsv) but only in aggregated form: distances.tsv carries a set pair and a
  distance with no coordinates, so it cannot be restricted to a gene subset after
  the fact. The unrestricted three-panel figure is fig_ecdf_heatmap_isect.pdf,
  drawn by scripts/make_ecdf_heatmap.R from hiconf_isect/.

Rebuild
  python3 overlap_shared_recompute.py
  Rscript make_figure_overlap_shared.R

Checks worth quoting if a reviewer asks
  Within-gene versus chromosome-only matching is identical at 50 nt: mean
  containment 0.0772 either way over all 20 ordered pairs, no pair inflated. The
  distance distributions differ only at long range (median 16,111 nt within gene
  versus 15,633 nt by chromosome), so panel A uses the within-gene version.

Limits (from the notes file, worth carrying into the thesis text)
  - 334 shared genes is a small, non-random scope biased toward long, highly
    expressed, multi-isoform genes. Panel B is concordance within genes all five
    studies detected, not a genome-wide estimate.
  - Panel D's competitor row is circular for both models.
  - The 50 nt tolerance is inherited, not tuned; panel A shows the full
    distribution so its effect is visible.
