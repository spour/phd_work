Figure: the extended models as the fix for the baseline's blind spot
Methods, numbers, results, and the figure caption
====================================================================

The figure answers one question. On the genes where the 500 nt baseline provably cannot rank the
annotated 3' end first, does reading 2,500 nt of context resolve the gene? It is an
error-conditional analysis: the denominator is the baseline's own errors, not all genes, and the
candidates are the baseline's own shortlist.


PART I   METHODS
================

I.1  THE TASK, AND WHY IT IS FRAMED ON THE BASELINE'S ERRORS
-------------------------------------------------------------
The extended models are re-rankers, not detectors, and the figure must not be read as a detection
claim. Ranking the annotated 3' end against every 1 nt stride window of a gene, on 24 held-out
genes and 2,806,911 windows, gives:

    baseline random forest    P@1 0.250    median rank of the annotated end       16
    extended CNN              P@1 0.042    median rank                           386
    splice, 26 features       P@1 0.000    median rank                        14,683

The baseline was trained genome-wide; the extended models were trained on candidate sets and have
never seen genomic background. Used as detectors they lose, and by a wide margin. The productive
question is therefore not whether they can find the 3' end but whether, on the genes the baseline
gets wrong, they can fix it. That residual is what the figure measures.

I.2  THE CANDIDATE SET, AND WHY THE BASELINE SCORES EXACTLY ZERO ON IT
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Competitors come from the CONSENSUS shortlist of scripts/build_shortlist.py. Per gene, from a
crossfit scan in which no model saw that gene's chromosome:

    canonical region  = the gene's window(s) from Dominant_CPA_baseline_total.bed
    m_canonical       = MAX m_prob over windows overlapping that region, m in {rf, lr, cnn}
    shortlist         = windows where rf >= rf_canonical
                                  AND lr >= lr_canonical
                                  AND cnn >= cnn_canonical

Every competitor was required to score at least as high as the annotated end under all three 500 nt
baselines independently. Verified against shortlist_feat_all.parquet, 79,700 rows and 64,140
internal competitors:

    model   competitors scoring >= canonical     strictly >     within-gene top-1
    RF      64,140 of 64,140  (100.0000%)          99.93%           0.0000
    LR      64,140 of 64,140  (100.0000%)          99.92%           0.0000
    CNN     64,140 of 64,140  (100.0000%)          99.93%           0.0000

Within-gene win rate is 0.0014 rather than 0.0000 only because about 0.07% of competitors tie the
canonical rather than exceed it, and a tie counts 0.5.

Two consequences carry into the figure and neither may be dropped.

  The baseline is a floor pinned at zero by construction, not by weakness. Across the 4,847 genes
  of the primary set the annotated end is ranked LAST in 4,847 of 4,847. No analysis moves it, and
  the figure must say so rather than present an arranged result as a victory.

  Contested is not detector-specific. All three baselines give the identical 6,583 genes.
  Competitors preferred by only one of them were never retained, so a per-detector contested set
  cannot be reconstructed from what survives, and no per-detector table should be built.

I.3  THE TWO STRATA
--------------------
    15,560 genes with a canonical row in shortlist_feat_all.parquet
       8,977  uncontested, no competitor outranks the annotated end, baseline already right  57.7%
       6,583  contested, at least one does, baseline wrong with certainty                    42.3%

    baseline genome-wide P@1 = 0.5769

A separate and genuinely empirical number, not true by construction: scanning every 500 nt window
at 1 nt stride, the random forest's single highest-scoring window lands on the annotated end in
1,561 of 6,580 genes, 23.7%. That is the baseline's behaviour before any shortlist is formed.

I.4  THE TWO EXTENDED MODELS
-----------------------------
Sequence CNN, 2,500 nt of [-2000, +500) in transcript orientation, one-hot:

    conv 19, 4 -> 32 channels, BatchNorm, exp(clamp 8)      the layer whose filters are readable
    max pool 10
    conv 7, 32 -> 64, BatchNorm, ReLU
    max pool 5
    conv 5, 64 -> 96, BatchNorm, ReLU
    max pool 2
    flatten 96 x 25, dense 64, dense 1

    Receptive field 378 nt at stride 100 nt, 25 output bins, both computed from the layer
    parameters rather than asserted. Trained class-balanced, pos_weight = 8.945, so its output is
    calibrated to a 50/50 prior and not to the 11.4% base rate; only the within-gene ordering is
    used here, which that calibration does not affect.

Splice model, 26 features over the same span:

    25 bins of u1_dens4, U1 snRNA duplex sites at free energy >= 4.0 counted per 100 nt bin over
    [-2000, +500) and expressed per kilobase, plus spl_upstream_max_me3, the maximum MaxEntScan
    score3 in [-2000, 0). Ridge logistic by IRLS, gene-centred scores.

I.5  BOTH MODELS ARE OUT OF FOLD ON EVERY GENE
-----------------------------------------------
This is the methodological point that the first version of the analysis got wrong, and it is worth
stating plainly.

The CNN's scores come from cnn_wg_scores.npz, leave-one-chromosome-out over all 70,723 peaks. The
splice model's locked coefficients, splice_model_26.npz, were fitted on TRAIN genes, so applying
them yields in-fold scores on train genes and can be reported only on val and test. That restricted
the splice rows to 453 of 1,623 test genes, exactly those whose every competitor survived the APA
and terminal-exon filters, which are easier: the CNN scores 0.7726 there against 0.5471 elsewhere.
Comparing a combined score measured on 453 genes against a CNN measured on 1,623 would have been a
gene-set difference presented as a model difference.

The fix is splice26_loco.py: the same 26 features, the same ridge logistic by IRLS at the same
lambda, the same gene-centring, refitted leave-one-chromosome-out over all 48,394 analysis peaks.
Only the folds change. Gene-centring cannot leak across folds because a gene sits on one
chromosome. The refit reproduces the locked model on the test genes, win rate 0.6996 and P@1 0.4683
against 0.6993 and 0.4720 on record, correlating with the locked scores at r = +0.9955.

Every row below is therefore the same genes, both models out of fold, no subset.

I.6  SCORING AND METRICS
-------------------------
Per contested gene the candidate set is the annotated peak plus its competitors. Each model scores
those candidates, the scores are z-scored within the gene, and

    rescue rate = fraction of contested genes where the annotated end comes first
    combined    = the unweighted sum of the two within-gene z-scores

No genome scan is run and no score is recomputed; every score already existed. An IRLS logistic
combiner fitted on train and val was tested and dropped: it optimises log loss rather than top-1
and scored below both the z-sum and the CNN alone.

Genome-wide precision at 1 follows by

    lifted = uncontested_fraction + rescue x contested_fraction = 0.5769 + rescue x 0.4231

which assumes the baseline is right on every uncontested gene. That is true by the definition of
uncontested inside this framing, and it is a claim about ranking the baseline's own candidates, not
about detection.

I.7  THE TWO CANDIDATE SETS
----------------------------
    Set A, the analysis set. The gene's canonical peak plus the competitors that survived the APA
    and terminal-exon filters. 4,847 contested genes, median 4 candidates, mean 9.98, maximum 898.
    Both models complete on every candidate of every gene, so set A is where the two extended
    models may be compared to each other.

    Set B, the full shortlist. Every competitor the baseline consensus kept, 6,583 contested genes.
    CNN only, because the 26 splice features were built for the analysis peaks. Set B is the honest
    number for the CNN across all contested genes.

The 1,736 contested genes in B but not in A are neither unscored nor harder. The CNN ranks the
annotated end first in 66.8% of them against 68.1% elsewhere. They are excluded because every one
of their competitors is an annotated polyadenylation site or a reference terminal exon, leaving no
contest once annotated competitors are removed.


PART II   RESULTS
=================

II.1  RESCUE RATE AND GENOME-WIDE LIFT
---------------------------------------
Set A, 4,847 genes, both models leave-one-chromosome-out, identical gene set on every row:

    model                 rescue rate    genome-wide P@1        gain
    baseline                 0.0000      0.5769                   --
    splice, 26 features      0.4630      0.5769 -> 0.7728     +19.6 points
    extended CNN             0.6812      0.5769 -> 0.8651     +28.8 points
    z-sum of the two         0.6942      0.5769 -> 0.8706     +29.4 points

Set B, 6,583 genes, CNN only:

    extended CNN             0.5772      0.5769 -> 0.8211     +24.4 points

The sequence CNN recovers the annotated 3' end in 68.1% of the genes the baseline cannot resolve,
lifting genome-wide precision at 1 from 0.577 to 0.865. Against the harder full shortlist it
recovers 57.7% and lifts to 0.821. The splice model alone recovers 46.3%, well above zero and well
below the CNN. Adding it to the CNN buys +1.3 points of rescue, 0.6812 to 0.6942, on the same genes
with no gene-set difference between the rows. Small, but clean.

II.2  RANK OF THE ANNOTATED END, NOT ONLY TOP-1
------------------------------------------------
Set A, 4,847 genes:

    model        rank 1     rank <= 2   rank <= 3   median rank   75th   90th
    baseline     0.0000       0.3351      0.4902          4         8      19
    splice       0.4630       0.7041      0.7991          2         3       7
    CNN          0.6812       0.8409      0.8892          1         2       4
    combined     0.6942       0.8378      0.8882          1         2       4

The baseline row is the shape of the construction: the annotated end is last in every gene, so its
rank distribution is exactly the distribution of candidate counts, and its median rank of 4 is the
median candidate count. The extended models move the median to 1 and leave the annotated end
outside the top three in 11.1% of genes.

II.3  WHICH MODEL DOES THE RESOLVING
--------------------------------------
Attribution over the 4,847 genes, by which model places the annotated site first:

    both models          1,787    36.9%
    sequence CNN only    1,515    31.3%
    splice only            457     9.4%
    neither              1,088    22.4%

The two models are not redundant. The splice model resolves 457 genes the CNN does not, 9.4% of the
contested set, which is why an either-model upper bound exists at all. It is also why the combined
z-sum, which must commit to one ordering, lands at 0.6942 rather than at the 0.7757 that choosing
the better model per gene would give.

II.4  THE FIGURE'S UNIVERSE AND ITS TOTALS
--------------------------------------------
Panel b draws set A inside the whole gene population, so that every ribbon closes:

    all genes        13,824   100.0%    = 8,977 resolved + 4,847 contested with both models scored
    baseline resolves 8,977    64.9%
    baseline fails    4,847    35.1%
      neither         1,088     7.9%
      splice only       457     3.3%
      sequence CNN only 1,515   11.0%
      both models     1,787    12.9%
    correct          12,736    92.1%
    incorrect         1,088     7.9%

    P@1  0.649 -> 0.921

The 0.921 is an upper bound: it assumes the better model is chosen per gene, which requires knowing
the answer. A single combined ranking, the within-gene z-sum, gives 0.893 on the same universe. The
0.649 differs from the 0.577 quoted for the full 15,560 genes only because this universe excludes
the 1,736 contested genes set A does not cover; both are stated so neither is mistaken for the
other.

II.5  WHAT THE FIGURE DOES NOT CLAIM
--------------------------------------
It does not claim detection. Over every window of a gene the same random forest reaches P@1 0.250
and the extended CNN 0.042 (I.1). It does not claim the baseline is a weak comparator on an even
footing: the baseline is at zero because the task is defined as its residual (I.2). It does not
claim the two extended models are interchangeable: they resolve overlapping but distinct gene sets
(II.3).


PART III   THE FIGURE
=====================

III.1  PANELS
--------------
(a) Sequence context read by each model. Two rows on a shared coordinate, nucleotides relative to
    the cleavage site. The upper row is the extended models' 2,500 nt span, [-2000, +500), carrying
    schematic splice sites. The lower row magnifies the baseline's 500 nt window, [-250, +250), so
    that the four core polyadenylation elements can be labelled at all; the magnification is the
    point of the panel, since the baseline reads a small neighbourhood in detail while the extended
    models read the whole span.

(b) Recovery of baseline-unresolved 3' ends. A four-column flow. Column 1 is the gene population,
    column 2 splits it into resolved and failed, column 3 attributes the failures to the model that
    fixes them, column 4 closes into correct and incorrect. Ribbon widths are gene counts and every
    ribbon closes within the drawn universe.

-------------------

6.6 inches. Blue #2A6DB0 marks the contested stream and the sequence CNN, dark blue #14395E the

III.3  CAPTION AS SET IN THE FIGURE
------------------------------------
The grey block below the panels reads:

  (a) The baseline scores ten core polyadenylation motifs, the polyadenylation signal in three
  variants, four downstream elements, UGUA, poly-U and NUDT, each scanned in 30 nt windows at
  stride 10 across a 500 nt span. The extended models read 2,500 nt, [-2000, +500) in transcript
  orientation, and add splice context and learned sequence. The magnified row is that 500 nt span;
  element positions within it are schematic, while the spans and the motif list are not.

  (b) Genes are contested when the baseline ranks at least one competitor at or above the annotated
  3' end, which it cannot avoid: build_shortlist.py retains a competitor only if the random forest,
  logistic regression and baseline CNN all score it at or above that site, so the baseline is never
  first on a contested gene. The universe is the 8,977 genes it resolves plus the 4,847 contested
  genes whose candidates both extended models score, 13,824 in total; within it every ribbon
  closes. Both models are leave-one-chromosome-out. Categories give which model places the
  annotated site first. The 0.921 assumes the better model is chosen per gene and is an upper
  bound; a single combined ranking, the sum of the two scores z-scored within each gene, gives
  0.893.

  Scope. Across all 6,583 contested genes, scored against their full candidate lists, the sequence
  CNN alone recovers 0.577 and lifts precision at 1 over all 15,560 genes from 0.577 to 0.821. The
  1,736 contested genes outside (b) are neither harder nor unscored, the CNN ranking the annotated
  site first in 66.8% of them against 68.1% elsewhere; they are excluded because every one of their
  competitors is an annotated polyadenylation site or a reference terminal exon, leaving no contest
  once annotated competitors are removed.

PART V   LIMITATIONS AND ONE OPEN QUESTION
==========================================
1  The splice model has no score for the 22,329 shortlist peaks outside the analysis set, so the
   1,736 contested genes whose competitors include APA-annotated or terminal-exon sites are
   untested for it. Closing that means building the 26 features for those peaks from hg38 directly
   rather than from cnn_data.npz, a sequence fetch plus a MaxEnt pass.

2  The lift arithmetic in I.6 credits the baseline with every uncontested gene. That is true inside
   this framing and false as a statement about genome-wide detection, where the same random forest
   reaches 0.250. The two must not be quoted in the same sentence.

3  Why the splice model, which reaches win rate 0.6996 on the analysis task, resolves only 46.3% of
   contested genes remains open. Two explanations were asserted and one was tested: candidate
   position is not the cause, since the shortlist is already 90% upstream and deleting the
   downstream candidates changes P@1 not at all. The smoothness hypothesis is untested and is
   labelled as such in splice_shortlist_open.txt.
