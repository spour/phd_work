JOURNAL-STYLE LEGEND (full)
================================================================================

Figure X. Reading 2,500 nt of context recovers the annotated 3' end on genes the
500 nt baseline cannot resolve.
(a) Sequence context read by each model. The baseline scores ten core
polyadenylation motifs, the polyadenylation signal in three variants, four
downstream elements, UGUA, poly-U and NUDT, each scanned in 30 nt windows at a
stride of 10 across a 500 nt span (48 bins per motif). The extended models read
2,500 nt, [-2,000, +500) in transcript orientation, and add splice context and
learned sequence. The upper bar is that 2,500 nt span with the 500 nt baseline
window shaded; the lower bar magnifies the shaded region so both can be labelled on
one axis. Element positions are schematic; the spans and the motif list are not.
The point of the panel is the difference in scale: the baseline reads a small
neighbourhood in detail, and only the extended window contains the upstream splice
sites.
(b) Recovery of baseline-unresolved 3' ends, drawn as a flow in which every ribbon
closes. A gene is contested when the baseline ranks at least one competitor at or
above the annotated 3' end, which it cannot avoid by construction: a competitor is
retained only when the random forest, the logistic regression and the baseline CNN
all score it at or above that site, so the baseline is never first on a contested
gene and its precision at 1 there is exactly zero. The universe is the 8,977 genes
the baseline resolves plus the 4,847 contested genes whose candidates both extended
models score, 13,824 in total; percentages are of that universe. Both extended
models are leave-one-chromosome-out on every peak, so every gene is ranked by fits
that never saw its chromosome. Of the 4,847 contested genes (35.1% of the universe),
the annotated 3' end is placed first by both models in 1,787 genes (12.9%), by the
sequence CNN alone in 1,515 (11.0%), by the splice model alone in 457 (3.3%), and by
neither in 1,088 (7.9%). Precision at 1 over the universe therefore rises from 0.649
to 0.921, from 8,977 to 12,736 genes ranked correctly. That 0.921 assumes the better
model is chosen per gene and is an upper bound; a single combined ranking, the sum of
the two scores z-scored within each gene, gives 0.893. Across all 6,583 contested
genes scored against their full candidate lists, the sequence CNN alone recovers
0.577 and lifts precision at 1 over all 15,560 genes from 0.577 to 0.821. The 1,736
contested genes outside panel b are neither harder nor unscored, the CNN ranking the
annotated site first in 66.8% of them against 68.1% elsewhere; they are excluded
because every one of their competitors is an annotated polyadenylation site or a
reference terminal exon, leaving no contest once annotated competitors are removed.
This is an error-conditional re-ranking result, not a detection claim: the
denominator is the baseline's own errors and the candidates are the baseline's own
shortlist.

The caveat this figure must be read with (from THESIS_figure_rescue.txt)
  The extended models are re-rankers, not detectors. Ranked against every 1 nt
  stride window of a gene, over 24 held-out genes and 2,806,911 windows, the
  baseline random forest gives P@1 0.250 (median rank 16), the extended CNN 0.042
  (median rank 386) and the splice model 0.000 (median rank 14,683). Used as
  detectors the extended models lose by a wide margin; the productive question, and
  the one this figure answers, is whether they fix the genes the baseline gets
  wrong.
