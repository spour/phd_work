JOURNAL-STYLE LEGEND (schematic as panel A, four panels re-lettered B-E)
================================================================================

Figure X. A sequence convolutional network outperforms splice features at
within-gene 3' end discrimination.
(A) Architecture. The network reads a 2,500 nt one-hot window (-2,000 to +500 nt
about the candidate cleavage site) through three convolutional blocks: 32 filters
of width 19 with batch normalisation and an exponential activation, which is the
layer whose filters are readable as position weight matrices, followed by max
pooling by 10; 64 filters of width 7 with batch normalisation and ReLU, pooled by
5; and 96 filters of width 5 with batch normalisation and ReLU, pooled by 2. The
result is 96 channels over 25 positional bins, giving each output bin a receptive
field of 378 nt at a stride of 100 nt. That map is flattened to 2,400 values, so
position is retained rather than pooled away, and passed through a 64-unit dense
layer (ReLU, dropout 0.4) to a single logit. Layer dimensions in the schematic are
computed from the layer parameters, not transcribed.
(B-E) Comparison against the 26-feature splice model, leave-one-chromosome-out over
the whole analysis set: 4,847 genes and 48,394 peaks (one real 3' end per gene and
43,547 intragenic competitors, base rate 0.100), 23 folds, every peak scored by a
fit that never saw its chromosome. Both models use the identical fold definition
and, inside each fold, a 25% gene holdout of that fold's training genes, on which
the splice model retunes its ridge penalty and the CNN early-stops; the two differ
in the model and in nothing else. Blue, sequence CNN; orange, splice model; bands
and error bars are 95% bootstrap intervals over genes.
(B) Pooled discrimination over all 48,394 peaks, ROC (left) and precision-recall
(right). The CNN reaches AUROC 0.815 and AUPRC 0.407 against 0.661 and 0.162 for
the splice model, on a base rate of 0.100. This panel is shown for reference only:
a competitor is a different site in the same gene, so a pooled metric rewards
separating genes rather than separating sites within one.
(C) Within-gene ranking, the primary comparison: the fraction of genes whose real
3' end falls in the top k candidates of that gene. Rank counts ties against the
model, so recall@1 is the strict precision at 1; chance (grey dashed) is the mean
over genes of min(k, candidates)/candidates, curved rather than linear because
genes differ in size. The CNN places the real 3' end first in 68.1% of genes
(95% CI 66.9-69.5%) against 46.5% (45.1-47.9%) for the splice model and 28.7% by
chance, and the gap persists across k (at k = 10, 97.4% against 94.2%, chance
90.6%). The corresponding within-gene win-rates are 0.847 (0.838-0.855) and 0.699
(0.689-0.710).
(D) Effect of dinucleotide shuffling. Each fitted model, unchanged and not
refitted, is applied to sequence shuffled across the whole window with the
Altschul-Erikson algorithm (3 replicates), which holds mononucleotide and
dinucleotide content exactly, so the global GC and CpG of each window are unchanged
and only local order and the placement of composition along the window are
destroyed. Filled bars, real sequence; open bars, shuffled; dashed line, chance.
The CNN falls from 0.847 to 0.629 (-0.218, 63% of its lift over chance) and the
splice model from 0.699 to 0.544 (-0.155, 78% of its lift). What survives is
therefore what global composition alone can do, 37% and 22% of the lift
respectively; the CNN's advantage rests more on order and position than the splice
model's does.
(E) Genome-wide identification of the used 3' end. Fraction of genes whose used 3'
end is ranked first, mapped over gene length and the number of candidate 3' ends
that gene carries, for the baseline peak caller and the two models on one shared
colour scale; each map covers all 15,475 genes with a GENCODE span (85 of 15,560
have none), each gene appears once, and a hexagon is drawn where at least 12 genes
fall in it. Genome-wide precision at 1 is 0.577 for the baseline, 0.747 for the
splice model and 0.821 for the CNN. A gene carrying a single candidate is ranked
first by any model, and all 8,977 such genes enter the denominator as correct for
the baseline as well, which is why the baseline is 0.577 rather than zero and why
it occupies the bottom row of every map; over the 6,583 contested genes alone,
where the baseline is 0 by construction, the splice model reaches 0.403 and the CNN
0.577. Length is what buys candidates, so how far each map stays blue upward and to
the right of that bottom row is what sequence adds. Contested genes are ranked over
their full candidate sets, 70,723 candidates in total, scored out of fold by the
same 23-fold scheme; no baseline probability enters either model as an input.

PROVENANCE
================================================================================

Top schematic
  make_figure_architecture.R  -> figure_architecture.pdf/.png
  Reads no data. Every dimension and the 378 nt receptive field are COMPUTED in the
  script from the layer parameters (kernel widths 19/7/5, pools 10/5/2), and
  asserted (RF == 378, stride == 100, 2500/100 == 25 bins), so the diagram cannot
  drift from cnn_model_def.py.
  The model itself: cnn_model_def.py (shared by cnn_train.py and cnn_interpret.py).

Bottom panels
  make_figure_loco.R          -> figure_loco_cnn_vs_splice.pdf/.png
  (computes nothing; re-asserts every number that carries the argument)
  Panel -> file -> producer
    A (pooled)     figLC_curves.tsv, figLC_summary.tsv   loco_figure_data.py
    B (recall@k)   figLC_recall.tsv                      loco_figure_data.py
    C (shuffle)    loco_shuffle.tsv                      loco_figure_data.py
    D (genome)     cnn_wholegenome_p1.tsv, wg_per_gene.tsv, wg_by_length.tsv
  CNN LOCO scores come from cnn_loco_train.py; the splice model is refitted inside
  loco_figure_data.py on the identical folds.

Rebuild
  python loco_figure_data.py
  /cvmfs/.../r/4.5.0/bin/Rscript make_figure_loco.R
  /cvmfs/.../r/4.5.0/bin/Rscript make_figure_architecture.R

Scope discipline the figure enforces in code
  - Two models only. The script asserts that no GC baseline, acceptor-alone arm or
    single-split score appears, and that panel C's splice arm is the 26-feature
    model, not the earlier 51-feature one (whose shuffle bars live in
    figure_cnn_vs_splice, a single-split comparison, and should not be quoted here).
  - No panel carries a subtitle; the explanatory text is in the caption block.

Numbers not to mix in
  The 0.7473 genome-wide P@1 for the splice model comes from
  splice_model_26_metrics.tsv and is the LOCO figure for THIS model; the same
  number quoted in older text for the 51-feature model is a different quantity.
