Does the polyadenylation CNN read splice sites?
Implantation, controls, and a search over sequence space
========================================================


1  QUESTION
-----------
A convolutional network trained to separate real 3' ends from competitor peaks reaches a within-gene
win rate of 0.821 and a pooled AUROC of 0.818. Splice sites are the strongest single sequence
feature in the surrounding biology: the acceptor alone separates the two classes at a win rate of
0.620 and an AUROC of 0.611. The question is whether the network uses them, and if not, what it uses
instead of them.

Attribution methods answer this only weakly, because a saliency map cannot distinguish a feature the
model reads from a feature correlated with one it reads. Implantation is causal: write a sequence
into a real genomic window, re-score, and the change is attributable to the sequence written.


2  METHODS
----------

2.1  MODEL AND DATA
The reported model is the one this project trains in cnn_train.py, a convolutional network over a
2,500 nt window centred 2,000 nt downstream of the window start, saved as cnn_model.pt at epoch 6.
Training used BCEWithLogitsLoss with pos_weight = n_negative / n_positive = 8.945, dropout 0.4,
learning rate 1e-3, weight decay 1e-6, batch 128, early stopping with patience 10.

The analysis set is 48,394 peaks, split by GENE so no gene contributes to more than one split:

  train  27,658    2,781 real 3' ends   24,877 competitors
  val     6,538      443 real 3' ends    6,095 competitors
  test   14,198    1,623 real 3' ends   12,575 competitors

Competitors are filtered baseline-model false positives, that is, peaks the upstream model called
that are not real 3' ends. Every number below uses the test split only, and uses ALL of it: the
12,575 are every held-out competitor, not a subsample. Windows containing an N token are excluded;
in the test split this removes none.

CALIBRATION CAVEAT, which governs how the probability axis may be read. Because training was class
balanced by pos_weight, the network's output is calibrated to a 50/50 prior rather than to the 11.4%
prevalence of real 3' ends. An untouched competitor therefore has a median predicted probability of
0.6251, not something near 0.114. Probabilities here are the raw sigmoid of the network's own logit
and are a relative scale, not posterior probabilities that a peak is a real 3' end. Ranking results
are unaffected, since win rate and AUROC depend only on the order of scores. The checkpoint also
stores a temperature of 1225.80 from a validation fit; dividing logits by that would collapse every
prediction onto 0.5, so the fit is degenerate and is not applied anywhere in this work.

2.2  IMPLANTATION ASSAY
A construct is written over the native bases at a fixed position 230 nt upstream of the cleavage
site, in every window of the chosen background, and the window is re-scored. The reported quantity
is either the mean change in logit or the median predicted probability across windows. Tandem dose
series repeat the unit n times, so the insert grows with n and displaces that much native sequence.

Two backgrounds are used. Real 3' end windows (n = 1,623) are used for the searches, since that is
where a 3' end signal should operate. Competitor windows (n = 12,575) are used for the reported
figure, for two reasons: real 3' ends already sit at a median probability of 0.952 and an activating
construct has no room to rise there, and "does adding a splice site to a competitor make the model
call it real" is the more direct question.

Earlier versions of this assay reported the best of 60 implantation positions. That was dropped.
The maximum is an upward-biased statistic taken separately for each construct and each control, and
it was shown to both inflate and mask effects; every number below is at a single fixed position.

2.3  NEGATIVE CONTROLS
Two, and both are reported, because neither is flat everywhere and choosing one after seeing the
result would hide that.

  MONONUCLEOTIDE SHUFFLE (20 draws). A permutation of the insert's own bases. Holds length and base
  composition exactly and varies only the order. Answers: would any sequence of these letters do
  this?

  DERANGEMENT (all 9). Every base mapped to a different base under a fixed permutation of {A,C,G,T}
  with no fixed point, applied to the whole insert. Holds length and position and nothing else.

The dinucleotide shuffle used in earlier rounds was abandoned: for a poly-pyrimidine construct it
returned the motif itself in 10 of 10 draws, so it was not a control at all.

The two disagree in an informative way. For the donor the derangement is much the flatter, staying
within 0.015 of baseline across all eight doses while the shuffle drifts to 0.564. For the acceptors
it is the reverse. Agreement between them indicates arrangement; disagreement localises the effect
to composition.

2.4  DERIVING THE IMPLANTED SEQUENCES
Nothing implanted is a consensus string from a database.

DONOR, CAGGTGAGT. The plain argmax of the U1 snRNA duplex free energy over all 4^9 = 262,144 9-mers,
decoded from the precomputed landscape and asserted in the pipeline rather than trusted. Note it is
NOT the textbook donor: the consensus string carried in cnn_interp_lib is CAGGTAAGT, and the
physical optimum differs from it at one position.

ACCEPTOR, TTTAACTTTCTTTTTTACAGACG. Derived from THE MODEL, not from an external measure. Greedy hill
climbing over single-base substitutions maximising the mean change in logit over 256 real 3' end
windows: at each step all 21 free positions by 3 alternatives are evaluated, the best is accepted,
and the search stops when none improves. Constraints are enforced at EVERY step so the search never
leaves the feasible set: AG pinned at positions 19 and 20, MaxEntScan score3 at least 10, the 18 nt
tract at least 80% pyrimidine, and no G anywhere in the tract. Three restarts from random feasible
seeds. Each arm is therefore a LOWER bound, since three greedy restarts do not exhaust 4^21
sequences.

2.5  DERIVING THE POSITION WEIGHT MATRICES
Both logos use one recipe with the measure swapped, which is the comparison they are there to make:
keep every sequence whose own measure lies within a threshold of that measure's optimum, weight each
linearly by closeness, weight = value - (optimum - threshold), then count weighted bases by position.

  DONOR. Exhaustive. All 262,144 9-mers are already scored, so the 115 within 2 kcal/mol of the
  optimum are kept. A physical cut rather than a rank cut, so 115 is a result and not a choice. GT
  was not pinned; it appears anyway at intron positions 1 and 2, which is the check that the recipe
  recovers a known motif without being told to.

  ACCEPTOR. The 23 nt register has 4^21 feasible sequences, so the ensemble is SAMPLED by
  Metropolis-Hastings rather than enumerated: target exp(delta / temperature) restricted to the same
  feasible set as the search, proposal one random feasible single-base substitution, 128 chains for
  900 steps with the first 200 discarded. 668 distinct sequences within 0.272 of the best delta
  found, consensus TTTTTCTCTTTTTTTTACAGCGT at a mean 1.25 bits per column.

Sampling temperature must scale with the height of the peak. A fixed 0.08 held the chains on the
unconstrained acceptor, whose optimum is near +1.85, but on the constrained one at +0.82 the same
value gave 0.70 acceptance, the chain mean fell from +0.80 to +0.10, and only 7 distinct near-optimal
sequences were recovered, which is a single sequence drawn as a logo. Temperature is now set from the
seed's own delta, giving 0.035 here and an acceptance of 0.09.

2.6  STATISTICS
Confidence intervals on the median probability are 95% percentile bootstraps over windows, 2,000
resamples. Control bands are the range spanned by the two control families, each plus or minus the
standard error of its own mean over draws; the standard deviation across individual draws is larger
and is reported separately. Permutation tests use 500 permutations of a sequence's own bases and
report the empirical p and a z against that null. No sklearn or scipy is used; AUROC, logistic
regression and Wilcoxon are implemented inline.


3  RESULTS
----------

3.1  ACCEPTORS CHOSEN BY MAXENTSCAN DO NOTHING
The MaxEntScan score3 optimum, TTTCCTCTCTCTCCCCGCAGGTG at score3 17.17, is the best 23 nt acceptor
that exists by that measure. Implanted into real 3' end windows it moves the logit by -0.138, and
against 500 permutations of its own bases it sits at z = -0.5 with p = 0.66. It is statistically
indistinguishable from a shuffle of itself.

3.2  WHERE A POSITIVE EFFECT WAS FOUND, IT WAS COMPOSITION
A T-rich acceptor, TTTTTTTTTGTGTGTTTTAGGTT at score3 14.38, gives a visible raw effect of +0.221.
Its own shuffles give +0.321. The intact sequence sits 0.100 BELOW a random reordering of its own
letters: z = -0.9, p = 0.79. The gain belonged to the bases and survived reordering them.

The two controls disagree in sign here, which is the diagnosis. Against derangements the same
sequence sits +0.357 ABOVE, because substitution turns a T tract into an A, C or G tract and the
model dislikes all three, so the substitution control pays the sequence for its composition. The
disagreement widens with dose, reaching +1.211 against -0.908 at seven copies.

3.3  SEARCHING FOR WHAT THE MODEL WANTS FINDS A DIFFERENT ACCEPTOR
Inverting the question and searching FOR model score under a score3 floor of 10 returns
ACGGTGTACTTTTGTTGCAGGTG at score3 10.04, worth +1.880, roughly fourteen times the score3 optimum and
in the opposite direction. Against 500 permutations of its own bases it reaches z = +15.4, with 0 of
500 draws matching it. All three independent restarts converged on the pentamer TGTAC.

Saturation mutagenesis localises it. Every costly position lies in 1 to 13 and nothing in 17 to 23.
Scrambling the TGTAC costs 1.58 of the 1.88; replacing it with AAAAA costs 1.66; a single change
TGTAC to TGCAC costs 0.87.

3.4  THE AG IS LARGELY DISPENSABLE, AND CAN BE MADE TO MATTER ONLY AT A PRICE
For that sequence, mutating the invariant AG to AC costs -0.034, that is nothing, while score3
collapses from 10.04 to 1.98. Scoring all sixteen dinucleotides at positions 19 to 20 shows AG
ranking 7th of 17 options including deletion, with six alternatives beating it.

A search that explicitly required every way of destroying the AG to cost score returns
CCATGCCTTTGATCCTGCAGATG at score3 10.38, where AG ranks 1 of 17 and nothing beats it. This is the
only sequence in the project for which that holds. The margin is small, +0.098 over the next best
dinucleotide, and the price is steep: activation falls from +1.880 to +0.567, a trade of roughly
three to one.

3.5  THE BETTER THE ACCEPTOR BY MAXENTSCAN, THE LESS THE MODEL WANTS IT
Repeating the search under progressively stricter definitions of "still an acceptor":

  arm         sequence                    delta    score3   tract pyr   tract G
  floor10     GTCCTTTTGTACCCCCGTAGGTT    +1.137    10.10      0.78       0.17
  floor12     TAACTGCTGTGTTCCTGCAGGTG    +1.288    12.06      0.67       0.22
  floor14     TGTTTTTCTGTCTTTTGCAGATG    +0.550    14.36      0.83       0.17
  floor16     TTTCTTCTCTCTCCCTGCAGGTG    +0.049    16.07      0.94       0.06
  pyr80       CGTTTTTTCTTTTTTTACAGACG    +0.779    10.71      0.89       0.06
  pyr80_noG   TTTAACTTTCTTTTTTACAGACG    +0.830    10.28      0.83       0.00

Two results. First, the Gs are not the point: forcing the tract to at least 80% pyrimidine with no G
at all still returns +0.830, from a sequence that reads like a textbook acceptor. Second, score3 IS
the point, and inversely: raising the floor toward the MaxEnt optimum collapses the best achievable
model score to +0.049.

The mechanism is C. A polypyrimidine tract is C or T and score3 treats them as near
interchangeable, so a high score3 forces the tract toward C. This model does not treat them as
interchangeable: in the exhaustive 6-mer scan, C runs are the most repressive 6-mers of all 4,096
and T runs are among the most activating. Read through that scan:

  unit                       C    T   C/(C+T)   6-mers negative   mean percentile
  floor16, score3 16.07      8    9    0.47        13 of 18             32
  pyr80_noG, score3 10.28    3   12    0.20         0 of 18             91
  MaxEnt optimum, 17.17     10    7    0.59        18 of 18             15

The floor-16 sequence is 94% pyrimidine, which is what makes it look like a textbook acceptor, but
nearly half of those pyrimidines are C.

3.6  THE REPORTED PANEL
Implanted into all 12,575 held-out competitor windows, whose untouched median predicted probability
is 0.6251:

  3' splice site  TTTAACTTTCTTTTTTACAGACG, score3 10.28, tract 83% pyrimidine, no G
    1 copy   0.799  [0.792, 0.805]      shuffles 0.642    derangements 0.596
    2        0.932  [0.930, 0.935]      shuffles 0.676    derangements 0.579
    3        0.975  [0.975, 0.976]      shuffles 0.689    derangements 0.560
    4        0.985  [0.985, 0.986]      shuffles 0.706    derangements 0.538

  5' splice site  CAGGTGAGT, the strongest U1 duplex 9-mer
    1 copy   0.593  [0.584, 0.604]      shuffles 0.620    derangements 0.626
    4        0.451  [0.439, 0.462]      shuffles 0.593    derangements 0.624
    8        0.331  [0.320, 0.341]      shuffles 0.564    derangements 0.610

The two run in opposite directions by comparable amounts, +0.36 and -0.29 in probability, while both
control families stay near the untouched baseline. The intervals are narrow, roughly 0.001 at four
acceptor copies, because the sample is every held-out competitor rather than a subsample.

3.7  HOW FAR THE DOSE SERIES CAN BE READ
Carrying the units to 24 copies shows the logit never saturates: the acceptor gains about +1.6 per
copy throughout, reaching +30.97 at 24 copies with its derangement control tracking within 0.34.
What fails is interpretation, in two ways.

Strong activators exhaust the probability scale and then leave the data. Median probability passes
0.999 at three copies. The share of windows whose logit exceeds the highest value the model emits on
any held-out sequence, +16.60, crosses 5% at seven copies, 39.7% at eight, 83.3% at nine and 100% at
fourteen.

Weak units never saturate because they never arrive; they are overtaken by length. The T-rich
acceptor peaks at three copies and falls to -1.111 by twelve, while its derangement control keeps
climbing. A separate homopolymer series shows why: past about 46 nt every homopolymer lands between
-0.4 and -2.0 regardless of base, and at 161 nt poly-T is MORE repressive than poly-C, the reverse
of their order at length 6.

Both failure modes bite by about four copies, which is where the reported series stops.


4  LIMITATIONS
--------------
The searches are greedy with three restarts over 4^21 sequences, so every reported optimum is a
lower bound; a run at floor 10 returned +1.137 where an earlier run with different seeds returned
+1.880, and the trend across constraints is the claim rather than any single value.

Implantation measures what the model does with a sequence written into a context, not what it does
with sequences as they occur. A construct displaces native bases, which is why every comparison is
against a length-matched control rather than against the unmodified window alone.

The probability axis is not calibrated, for the reason given in 2.1.

The effect of the acceptor found here is real by permutation but modest in absolute terms, and the
AG contributes little to it. The honest summary is that this model can be made to respond to an
acceptor-shaped sequence, that its response is driven by pyrimidine identity rather than by
splice-site strength, and that it runs opposite to MaxEntScan across the range where MaxEntScan
discriminates.
